1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose of the document
This document presents the Proposal Document for the GraphMind (Enterprise Knowledge AI Platform) system — an enterprise knowledge management platform that applies GraphRAG technology to support decision-making for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). The document establishes the research context, problem statement, research objectives, methodology, proposed system architecture, and scope of development, and serves as the primary reference for consensus among the implementing student team, instructors/review panels, and stakeholders prior to the implementation of detailed design documents (SRS).
The main readers of the document include: instructors and the Capstone project evaluation board, the project development student group (C1SE.18 group), and stakeholders who play the role of hypothetical SMEs in case studies.
1.2 Scope of materials
This document covers the following contents: the context of research and analysis of fragmented data in SMEs; a literature overview of existing RAG methods; research objectives and expected scientific contributions; research methodology and proposed system architecture; the scope of the Minimum Viable Product (MVP); detailed description of the experimental dataset; evaluation methods and benchmarks; security measures; project implementation plan in each Sprint; and risks and mitigation strategies.
This document does not include: detailed use-case level software requirements specification (described in a separate SRS document), actual implementation source code, commercial deployment/operation agreements, or any contractual/financial terms — all of which are considered simulations for educational and research purposes.

2. Problem Statement
2.1 Research context
Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) are now operating with an increasing volume of internal knowledge but are severely fragmented across multiple data sources that differ in nature: on the one hand, structured data is housed in CRM/ERP systems — order recognition,  customers, products, suppliers and personnel; on the other hand, unstructured data is scattered in contracts, legal documents, and internal documents. This fragmentation makes it difficult for SME administrators to answer decision-making questions that require the simultaneous linking of both types of data, e.g., "Which customers have a cap on liability clause and at the same time have a decrease in sales in the most recent quarter?"
To study this issue in a controlled and reproducible manner, the team used two real-world datasets, publicly available, and explicitly licensed for educational/research purposes, without altering their original content or fabricating business data: (1) Microsoft's AdventureWorks, which serves as the structured CRM/ERP data of an SME that manufactures and distributes international bicycle manufacturing and distribution; and (2) CUAD (Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset) — which serves as an SME's unstructured contract repository, consisting of 510 actual commercial contracts labeled by legal experts under 41 types of clauses. The use of two published and verified benchmark datasets helps to ensure the objectivity, reproducibility and academic reliability of the research results, while avoiding biases that may arise from fabricated business data. The only synthetic element is the Controlled Synthetic Linking process (Section 8.5), which merely constructs cross-source relationships between independent AdventureWorks entities and CUAD contracts for experimental evaluation, and leaves the original data of both datasets unchanged.
Regarding the choice of the answer generation model, instead of calling the API of a closed-source commercial large language model, the project advocates self-fine-tuning an open-source language model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) on SME-specific data. This choice stems from three main reasons: (1) Data security — because SME data includes both contractual information and customer transactions is sensitive, model autonomy and inference infrastructure help prevent data from being sent externally via third-party APIs; (2) Cost — calling commercial APIs according to the number of tokens incurring long-term operating costs is not suitable for SMEs' limited resources, while the open-source model after fine-tuning can be self-hosted with almost zero marginal costs; (3) Customization — fine-tuning using LoRA/PEFT techniques on the AdventureWorks and CUAD datasets itself allows the model to learn the style, business terminology, and question structure specific to the SME business management field, which is difficult for a general base model to optimize well.
2.2 Business motivation
In actual operations, SME administrators often have to manually synthesize information from multiple discrete systems, leading to delays in decision-making, increasing the risk of missing hidden legal risks in contracts, and limiting the ability to extract value from available data. A knowledge management platform with unified query capabilities on both relational data (CRM/ERP) and text data (contracts) will help SMEs shorten decision-making time, reduce compliance risks, and improve competitiveness without requiring large infrastructure investments like large-scale enterprises.
2.3 Core research issues
The core question of the project is stated as follows: Whether a Hybrid GraphRAG query mechanism — combining knowledge graphs (Graphs), vector semantic search (Vectors) and large language models (LLMs) — will deliver higher answer accuracy, source citation accuracy, and the ability to reduce hallucinations than the Vector RAG and Pure GraphRAG baselines, especially for governance questions that require multi-hop inference on cross-source data between structured CRM/ERP data and unstructured contract data?
3. Literature Review
3.1 Vector RAG (Traditional RAG)
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines a retriever model with a large language model (LLM) to provide relevant context before the answer is generated, help reduce hallucination, and allow the LLM to respond based on up-to-date knowledge beyond the scope of training. The most common Vector RAG approach uses semantic embedding to represent text as a vector and search for the closest pieces of text semantically in a vector database. The advantages of this method are simple, easy to implement and effective with single-hop event lookup questions; However, it is markedly limited when the question requires aggregating information from multiple interrelated entities, due to the nature of the search based on local similarity without capturing the relational structure between entities.
3.2 GraphRAG
GraphRAG extends RAG by building a knowledge graph that represents business entities (customers, products, orders, contracts, employees, etc.) and the relationships between them, and then performs retrievals through graph traversals instead of relying solely on vector similarity. This approach allows the model to answer multi-hop questions that require inference across multiple linked entities, while improving the ability to cite sources explicitly thanks to the graph's clear relational structure. However, Pure GraphRAG may be limited when dealing with free-form semantic queries, without a clear relational structure in the graph, or when the graph schema does not fully cover the concepts that appear in the unstructured text.
From the above two observations, the Hybrid GraphRAG approach—which combines knowledge graphs in tandem, semantic vector search, and the natural language synthesis capabilities of LLMs—is expected to take advantage of the advantages of both methods: the relational inference accuracy of graphs and the semantic flexibility of vector search.
3.3 Research gaps
#
Research Gap
Description
1
Lack of cross-source assessment on real data
The majority of GraphRAG research currently evaluates on a single type of data (either plain text, or pure relation), lacking a benchmark that combines structured CRM/ERP data and an unstructured contract repository as the actual operating context of SMEs.
2
Lack of fair comparison of three methods on the same benchmark
Current publications typically only compare GraphRAG with Vector RAG, rarely evaluating both Vector RAG, Pure GraphRAG and Hybrid GraphRAG simultaneously on the same set of questions divided by difficulty (single-hop, 2-hop, cross-source).
3
Lack of transparent, reproducible data linkage process
When combining multiple independent benchmark datasets (without native link keys), most studies do not clearly disclose the pairing process, making it difficult to replicate the results.
4
Lack of contextual assessment of small-scale SMEs
Current GraphRAG research is primarily aimed at large enterprises or technology corporations; there is a lack of feasibility and implementation cost assessment studies in the context of SMEs with limited resources.

6. Research Methodology and System Architecture
6.1 Overall system architecture
The GraphMind system is designed according to 6 main modules, closely coordinating to realize both the research track and the system track:
Module 1 — Data Source & Sync Management: manages connection channels and synchronization for all data sources.
– Connection management: configure and authenticate Google Drive / Google Sheets accounts via OAuth 2.0.
– AEGIS Local Agent Panel: manage the list of workstations with the Agent installed, monitor Online/Offline status and watched folders (File Watcher).
– Sync orchestration & scheduling: configure real-time or periodic sync mode, trigger Incremental Sync.
– Data Sync & Audit Log: track ingestion history (files ingested, records successfully extracted, error messages) with system metadata stored securely in PostgreSQL. 
Module 2 — Knowledge Management & Editor: lets business users browse and curate the extracted knowledge.
– Document & Data Explorer: browse ingested documents (contract PDFs, order/customer Excel files) with File Preview.
– Human-in-the-loop Knowledge Editor: view AI-extracted entities (Customer, Product, Contract) and relationships; allow users to manually edit nodes and add/remove links when the AI extraction is inaccurate.
– SOP & Internal Knowledge Base: a built-in text editor for managers to author internal process guides and policies directly on the Web.
Module 3 — Hybrid AI Copilot & Search: the core query and reasoning experience, combining graph traversal, vector search and the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model.
– Multi-turn Chat Copilot: natural-language Q&A over the enterprise's full business and legal data.
– Knowledge Graph Visualizer: display the network of nodes/edges directly relevant to a question so users can verify the AI's reasoning.
– Source & Citation Viewer: click a citation to open the underlying Sheets row or the original contract PDF passage.
– Session & Workspace Management: manage chat history and pin important Q&A sessions as shared team documents.
Module 4 — Decision Support & Alerts: turns the underlying knowledge graph into executive-ready insight.
– Executive Decision Dashboard: visual charts of aggregated business data (revenue over time, top high-value customers), contract-type distribution and clause-compliance rate.
– Smart Risk & Alert Center: list of contracts expiring in the next 15–30 days; alerts for VIP customers showing declining order frequency.
– Executive Briefing Generator: a one-click button for the AI to automatically summarize the week's/month's business, receivables and contract status into a PDF/Word file.
Module 5 — Enterprise Multi-facet Search: fast, unified lookup across all business entities.
– Unified search bar: a single keyword search across Customers, Products, Contracts and documents simultaneously.
– Multi-facet Filters: filter by responsible department, creation date, transaction value, and document validity status.
Module 6 — System Admin & Security: centralizes user administration, access control and AI operations monitoring.
– User & Organization Management: manage staff records, grouped by department (Sales, Accounting, Board of Directors, IT). All user accounts and organizational structures are managed via a PostgreSQL relational database.
– Granular RBAC: configure the scope of knowledge each role may query (e.g., Sales staff may only query Orders/Customers; Directors have full query access).
– LLMOps & Cost Dashboard: track fine-tuning training runs, GPU usage and training time (Kaggle GPU sessions), model checkpoints (Hugging Face Hub and Unsloth), and inference latency (average response time), alongside estimated compute usage.

6.5 Hybrid Query Mechanism
When receiving a question from a user, the hybrid query engine follows the following process: (1) analyzing the question to identify related business entities (Customer, Product, Contract, etc.); (2) implementing graph traversal on Neo4j to retrieve directly related entities and relationships, especially effective with multi-step queries; (3) in parallel perform a vector semantic search on Qdrant to retrieve contractual texts that are semantically related but not necessarily explicit in the graph; (4) consolidate (fuse) results from the two channels of retrieval, ranking according to relevance; (5) Feed the consolidated context into a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model to generate a final answer with a clear source citation to both the CRM/ERP data sheet and the relevant contractual clause. In summary, the query flow is: User Query → Question Analysis → Neo4j Graph Traversal → Qdrant Vector Search → Result Fusion → Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct → Answer + Source Citations. Neo4j (graph database, used for relationship/entity traversal) and Qdrant (vector database, used for semantic similarity retrieval) are retrieval components rather than AI models; the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is the sole generative model, responsible for answer generation and reasoning.
6.6 Deployment Architecture
The system applies a hybrid deployment architecture: AEGIS Local Agent (File Watcher) is installed locally on the SME's internal server, which is responsible for monitoring the local files/folders, detecting changes, and performing incremental synchronization by sending the changes over a secure connection to the Backend Cloud (FastAPI), which is located on the cloud infrastructure, receives the synchronized data, and manages knowledge processing and retrieval. This approach allows SMEs to keep sensitive data (such as original contracts) in their internal systems while leveraging the computing power and scalability of the cloud platform for AI query tasks.

8.3 Fine-tuning Data Construction (Q&A Pairs)
In order to fine-tune the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model for SMEs, the team build a dataset of Q&A pairs based entirely on the actual content of AdventureWorks and CUAD (after performing controlled synthetic linking — see Section 8.5). The process of generating Q&A pairs was carried out in three groups corresponding to the three levels of inference of the benchmark:
Single-hop: a question that looks up an entity directly from AdventureWorks (e.g., order quantity, product information) or a specific term from CUAD.
2-hop: a question that requires linking two directly related entities in the same data source (e.g., Customer → Order → Product, or Contract → Clause).
Cross-source: a question that requires combining information from both AdventureWorks and CUAD via synthetic linking (e.g., a relationship between the transaction history and the terms of the same customer's contract).

8.5 Data Integration: Controlled Synthetic Linking
Since AdventureWorks and CUAD are two independent benchmark datasets with no natural join keys between them, the team implemented a controlled synthetic linking process to simulate the actual operational context of an SME, in which each customer has both a transaction history (AdventureWorks) and a corresponding commercial contract (CUAD). The process is carried out as follows:
Each Customer in AdventureWorks is randomly and controllably assigned to 1–3 contracts from CUAD, based on sound business logic (e.g., a customer whose component/product purchase is assigned to a supply contract or a service contract of a corresponding nature).
Linking mapping is fully documented as a standalone lookup table, separate from the original data of both datasets, ensuring that the original data is not altered.
All source code, random parameters (seeds) and assignment rules are published with the project to ensure full transparency and reproducibility of the linking process.
The linking is only for the purpose of facilitating cross-source queries in the Q&A fine-tuning data generation and evaluation benchmark; The contents of each data set (AdventureWorks transactions, CUAD contract terms) themselves remain completely authentic and unaltered.
This approach allowed the team to build a controlled inter-source benchmark while retaining the academic integrity and reliability of both original datasets.

13. Expected Outcomes and Significance
Scientific significance
The project hopes to provide quantitative, reproducible empirical evidence of the efficacy of Hybrid GraphRAG compared to Vector RAG and Pure GraphRAG on a new cross-source benchmark that combines structured and unstructured data in an SME context. The findings contribute to closing the gaps identified in Section 3.3, while providing a transparent controlled synthetic linking process that can be reused by future researchers who want to combine multiple independent benchmark datasets.
Practical significance
The MVP GraphMind platform illustrates the feasibility of applying Hybrid GraphRAG in the context of SMEs with limited resources, through a hybrid deployment architecture (Local Agent + Cloud) that balances internal data security and the ability to leverage AI capabilities in the cloud. The product has the potential to serve as a reference platform for practical enterprise knowledge management solutions, helping SME administrators shorten the time to retrieve inter-source information and minimize the risk of missing important contractual clauses when making decisions.
Independent Reviewer Assessment
To support the team’s own critical review process, the assessment below was produced with AI assistance, adopting the perspective of an independent capstone reviewer. It is included as a self-critique to strengthen the proposal rather than as an endorsement from a named external expert, and its use should be reflected in the project’s AI Usage Disclosure Statement (Sprint 4 deliverable, Section 12).
Criteria
Assessment
Practical / Commercial Value
Addresses a genuine, well-documented SME pain point (siloed structured and unstructured knowledge) and grounds it in two independently licensed, verifiable public datasets rather than fabricated business data, which supports academic credibility. Commercial readiness is nonetheless limited: (a) the “controlled synthetic linking” between AdventureWorks and CUAD is an artificial construct with no equivalent in a real SME’s data, so cross-source findings cannot be generalized to a paying customer without re-validation; (b) a self-hosted 7B fine-tuned model is unlikely to match frontier closed models on ambiguous natural-language queries; and (c) go-to-market concerns (pricing, onboarding, compliance certifications) are explicitly out of scope. As positioned, the project’s strongest value is as a research prototype and technical proof-of-concept, not yet as a deployable commercial product.
Scope Assessment (breadth)
The proposal combines a full empirical research track (dataset linking, Q&A generation, LoRA fine-tuning, three-baseline benchmarking, a five-metric evaluation framework) with a full system track (six modules, including a chat copilot, knowledge-graph viewer, local file-watcher agent, RBAC, optional Google Drive/Sheets integration, and an LLMOps cost dashboard) for five students within roughly 13 weeks (01/09/2026 – 01/12/2026). Relative to that team size and timeline, the scope is too broad rather than too narrow. Section 7 already trims several items (mobile app, multi-tenant support, commercial ERP integration), which is a sound instinct, but could go further: Google Drive/Sheets integration, the Executive Briefing Generator, and the LLMOps & Cost Dashboard could be deferred to a “stretch” backlog without weakening the core scientific contribution, freeing time to harden the Hybrid Query Engine and the evaluation pipeline that the research claims actually depend on.
Methodology Assessment
The design is generally sound: using two independently published, license-cleared datasets instead of fabricated business data avoids a common validity threat in GraphRAG literature, and comparing three baselines on an identical, difficulty-stratified benchmark directly targets the gaps the team itself identified in Section 3.3. Combining LLM-as-judge with human validation is an appropriate way to reduce automated-scoring bias. Two risks stand out — both already flagged by the team in Section 14 rather than overlooked: (i) the synthetic customer–contract linking is a threat to external validity that no amount of documentation fully removes, since it can only be checked internally; and (ii) a 100–200-question benchmark, evaluated in part by the same five-person team that built the system, is a fairly small, potentially non-blinded sample for the confidence-level claims the Technical Report will likely want to make — an independent or blinded validation pass on a subsample would strengthen the evidentiary basis of the results.
Overall Feasibility Score
7.5 / 10 — strong problem framing, a defensible dataset choice, and a methodologically fair three-baseline design, held back by an overloaded scope for the team size and timeline and by validity limits inherent to the synthetic data-linking approach.
